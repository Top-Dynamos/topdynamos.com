The problem

Many physicians have more public information than they realize, spread across hospital pages, practice sites, directories and old articles. Little of it agrees, and almost none of it tells your whole story.

  • A career in pieces

    Medical school on one site, residency on another, your current practice somewhere else. Nobody sees the whole path.

  • An outdated biography

    The hospital page was written when you joined. It still lists an old title, an old focus or a partner who left years ago.

  • Credentials shown wrong

    Directories copy each other. A missing board certification or a wrong specialty can repeat across a dozen sites.

  • Practice and hospital disconnected

    Your practice site lists services, the hospital lists one line, and listing sites show an address you left.

  • Accomplishments hard to find

    Research, teaching, military service and community work sit in your CV, where patients and referring doctors never see them.

  • Too little for a patient to go on

    A patient choosing a specialist, or a doctor making a referral, finds a name and a phone number, and little that explains who you are.

Why it matters

The decision to call your office often starts with a search for your name.

  1. 1

    A patient is referred to you

    A patient may look you up before booking, even when their own doctor sent them.

  2. 2

    They read the first screen

    They see what directories, review sites and old pages say, and form a view in seconds.

  3. 3

    A referring doctor checks you too

    Colleagues look for training, focus and affiliations before they send patients your way.

  4. 4

    Clear information earns the call

    When the facts are complete and agree everywhere, there is less reason to hesitate.

The solution: one record, in the forms people use

We write one accurate account of your career from your documents, you approve it, and we publish it where people look. Every version says the same thing, so a search for your name leads to a clear and consistent picture.

What it looks like by specialty

Every field has its own blind spots. These are typical, not promises about any one person.

  • Cardiologists

    Common gap
    Certifications in internal medicine, cardiovascular disease and imaging, plus privileges at more than one hospital, rarely appear together in one place.
    What we do
    One biography lists each certification and affiliation as you document it, with your clinical focus in plain words a patient understands.

  • Family physicians and general practitioners

    Common gap
    Years of continuity care reduce to a short practice listing, with nothing about your approach, languages or the patients you see.
    What we do
    A personal website and profile explain who you care for, how you work and where you practice, in your own approved words.

  • Podiatrists

    Common gap
    Many patients do not understand podiatric training or foot and ankle surgical certification, and listings rarely explain it.
    What we do
    Your biography sets out your degree, residency and certifications clearly, so patients understand your training before they visit.

  • Surgeons and proceduralists

    Common gap
    Fellowship training and procedure focus are buried, so patients cannot tell what you do most.
    What we do
    We describe your training and focus areas factually, without outcome claims or comparisons.

  • Academic physicians

    Common gap
    Publications, grants and teaching sit on a faculty page that patients never read.
    What we do
    The research and teaching you choose appear on your site and profile, linked to the original sources.

  • Physicians in private practice

    Common gap
    Your practice site sells the practice. Nothing tells your own story as a physician.
    What we do
    A site under your own name sits beside the practice site and links to it, so both can be shared.

What we will not write

  • Patient information. Patient details stay private, and your site carries no patient testimonials.
  • Outcome claims. Your biography describes your training and work without claims of better results, success rates or comparisons with other doctors.
  • Unchecked credentials. A certification appears only when you can show it. A profile is not a license check.
  • Paid recognition as an honor. Any paid award or certificate is labeled as part of a paid service, for clients who meet eligibility.

How publishing works

Seven steps, the same for every client. You approve the text before anything is published or printed.

  1. 1

    Information

    You send your CV, licenses or bar details, education, positions, publications and a photo. A short call fills the gaps.

  2. 2

    Research and drafting

    We check your details against your documents and public sources, then write the biography, release and site pages.

  3. 3

    Your review

    You read every word. Mark anything that is wrong, missing or not how you would say it.

  4. 4

    Approval

    You approve the final text in writing. Nothing goes out before that.

  5. 5

    Publication

    The approved work goes live: website, registry profile and press release, as your package includes.

  6. 6

    Delivery

    Printed pieces in your package, such as a hardcover, or a certificate and award for clients who meet eligibility, are produced from the approved text and sent to you.

  7. 7

    Revisions

    If a fact changes later, tell us. How many updates are included is set out in your agreement.

Questions

Browse physician profiles in the registry

What do I need to send?

Your CV, details of your licenses and board certifications, your current practice and hospital affiliations, any publications, and a professional photo. If something is missing, we ask before we write.

Do you verify my credentials?

Yes, carefully. We check what we write against the documents you send and against public sources such as board certification lookups. Licenses themselves are confirmed by the state medical board, which is where patients can check them.

Will patients find me on Google?

We give search engines more accurate pages about you: a website under your name, a registry profile and a press release, so the right information is much easier to find. Search engines decide the order, so we report your progress every month.

Can my hospital or group object?

Some employers have rules about outside publicity. If yours does, check them first. We can match the wording your employer requires.

Can I include patient reviews?

You can link your site to the review pages you choose, so patients see genuine opinions. To protect your credibility and patient privacy, we never write, buy or arrange reviews or publish patient stories.

Who approves the final text?

You do. Nothing is published, printed or sent until you approve it in writing.
